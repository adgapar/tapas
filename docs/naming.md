# Tapas naming

Every tapa name is a real Spanish word that:

1. Sounds or looks like an English word close to the function, so English speakers read it without a translation.
2. Has a precise, literal Spanish meaning that matches what the tool does. Never decorative.

This is the opposite of two traps:

- Evocative vibe words (Granola, Kumis). Pretty, empty, need a brand story.
- Literal English (Dictate, Capture, Notes). Clear, generic, hard to own.

Clear and distinctive. Both at once.

> Every tapa has a name that reads naturally in English and means exactly what it does in Spanish. No invented words, no unrelated puns.

## Test for a new name

1. Does it look like an English root a user already knows? Dictado → dictate. Acta → act. Captura → capture. Consulta → consult.
2. Is it a real Spanish word, not invented? Non-negotiable.
3. Does that Spanish meaning describe the tapa's output or action, not a vibe? Acta is the minutes. Captura is the screenshot.
4. Short. Ideally 2–3 syllables, ending in -a or -o, sayable on first read with no guide.

## Categories

- Input/output actions → verbs as nouns: Dictado, Captura, Consulta
- Artifacts → the thing produced: Acta, Nota
- Features (not tapas) → same linguistic test, not on the plate. Orden is the router every hotkey take goes through.

One exception is allowed: skip the English-look rule if the Spanish meaning alone earns it. Mundo (world) for a multilingual layer. Exceptions stay rare.

## Features, not tapas

Same naming test. Not a dish. You do not pick them from the plate.

| Name | Kind | Looks English | Real Spanish | Precise meaning | Length | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Orden** | feature | order | an order, a command | every hotkey take is classified, then a tapa or job runs | 2, -n | Keep. Not a tapa. Ends in -n. Replaces Camarero. |

Do not use: Ojo (no English root), Busca (no English root), Clip (English, generic), Emo (not Spanish), Cara (the bezel face is chrome, not a tapa), Camarero (on the menu). Do not list Orden as a tapa.
