# Prepare for a conversation

Use when asked to prepare for a meeting, follow-up, or discussion using past Tapas
recordings. Identify the person, project, or topic and the conversation's purpose
from the request. If these are missing, ask for the minimum needed to find relevant
history. Do not assume calendar or contact access.

Search relevant Dictado and Acta recordings, read supporting passages, and state
coverage. Prefer recent context while retaining older unresolved decisions or
questions. Check later sources before presenting an old issue as current. Do not
infer named speakers from microphone or app-audio labels.

Produce a concise briefing: relevant background, confirmed decisions, commitments
and their known status, unresolved questions, and a suggested agenda. Cite the
historical claims. Separate proposed talking points from things already agreed.
Include useful contradictions or gaps instead of filling them with guesses. If
there is no relevant history, say so and offer an agenda based only on the user's
stated purpose, clearly labeled as a suggestion.

Return the briefing in the conversation, or save a new Markdown file under
`outputs/` when requested. Do not contact participants or schedule a meeting as
part of preparing the brief unless the user explicitly includes those actions.
